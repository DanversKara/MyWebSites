Notice: Why We Are Not Publishing Our App on Google Play

After careful consideration, we have decided that publishing our app on the Google Play Store is not the right choice for this project.

Our app is designed primarily for private, personal use. We are not building it as a commercial product, nor are we trying to operate a business through it. We also intend to make the source code publicly available as an open-source project, allowing people to access, review, and use the software without paying for it.

Unfortunately, Google's app publishing and testing requirements make this process unnecessarily difficult for small, independent developers and private projects.

For example, Google Play requires certain new personal developer accounts to complete a closed testing period involving at least 12 testers who must remain opted in for 14 consecutive days before applying for production access. Finding that many people willing to participate in testing can be difficult, especially when a project is intended for private use and does not have an established user base.

Google has introduced these requirements to help prevent scammers, malicious applications, and potentially harmful software from reaching users. We understand and respect the importance of protecting users. However, these requirements can also create unnecessary obstacles for legitimate independent developers, hobbyists, and people creating software for personal use.

Publishing through Google Play can also involve additional verification, administrative requirements, and potential costs, particularly for developers who want to operate commercially or distribute their applications as a business. For independent developers who simply want to share free, open-source software, these additional steps can be difficult to justify.

Since our project is intended for personal use, is open source, and is not designed to generate revenue, there is little benefit in going through Google's publishing process simply to distribute an application and its updates.

Instead, we have decided to distribute the application through alternative channels, including GitHub Releases and F-Droid, where appropriate.

These platforms provide ways to distribute software, share updates, and make open-source projects accessible without requiring us to go through Google Play's specific testing and publishing requirements.

Our decision is not about avoiding security or quality standards. It is about choosing distribution methods that better suit the purpose of this project.

We believe that free and open-source software should be accessible without unnecessary financial, administrative, or testing barriers, particularly when it is developed independently and provided for personal use.

Thank you for understanding and supporting independent, open-source development.

Before you install

  • You need an account on a PBX. The app connects to a PBX you run or have been invited to. There's no signup in the app.
  • Signing in. Open the app, check the Server address, tap "Sign in with my browser" and sign in. The app sets up your phone line by itself. Sign-in lasts 60 days after you last use the app.
  • Outside calls need a VoIP provider. Calls and messages between people on your PBX are free. Regular phone numbers, SMS/MMS, 911 and fax only work if your PBX has a VoIP provider (OwnPBX is tested with voip.ms; see the home page).
  • Don't rely on it for 911. Emergency calling hasn't been tested end to end. Keep a regular phone for emergencies (Terms, section 5).
  • Android only, for now. No Google services, no ads, no tracking.

Your data

The app talks only to your PBX. Ask for a copy of your data or delete your account at myaccount.ownpbx.org/ucp/delete (or with your own PBX's administrator). Details in the Privacy Policy.
