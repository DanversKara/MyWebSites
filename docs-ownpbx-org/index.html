Self-hosted · GPL-2.0-or-later

Your own phone system.

A self-hosted home/hobby phone system. Plain Asterisk 22 does the calling, a small Python service makes every call decision, a clean web panel runs the whole thing, and a separate Kamailio edge box is the only part that faces the internet.

Install own-pbx → Try the live demo Android app
  • Asterisk 22
  • Kamailio edge
  • rtpengine
  • Python call brain
  • SQLite
  • TLS + SRTP
Hobby project — read this first

own-pbx is built and tested as a home/hobby PBX. It is not intended for business, commercial, multi-tenant, carrier or life-safety use, and comes with no warranty. Only tested with VoIP.ms. 911/E911 has NOT been tested end to end — do not rely on this system to call 911. Stripe billing has not been tested with real payments. Published as-is under GPL-2.0-or-later.

Free on its own. Regular phone numbers need a VoIP provider.

Free without a provider: calls, messages, voicemail and push-to-talk between everyone on your PBX. Give a friend, family member or anyone, across the street or across the world, an extension: with the My Phone app (or any SIP app) and an internet connection, they can call and message you for free, privately, through your own server.

Needs a VoIP provider: calls to and from regular phone numbers, SMS/MMS, 911/E911 and fax. Without one, own-pbx is internal only.

own-pbx was built and tested with voip.ms. To try it, sign up with our voip.ms referral link ↗: voip.ms adds a bonus credit when you deposit $15 or more (check voip.ms for the current offer). It's a referral link, so we may also get a credit; own-pbx isn't affiliated with voip.ms.

#What you get

Calling →

Extensions, ring groups, IVR menus, conference room, voicemail by phone.

Phones anywhere →

Desk phones and softphones at home; encrypted TLS + SRTP through the edge box when away.

Texting →

Extension-to-extension texts plus real two-way SMS/MMS through voip.ms.

My Phone →

Every user gets calls, voicemail, recordings, texts, contacts with notes, IVR, billing and settings.

Android app →

Free, open-source My Phone app (signed releases on GitHub): texts, calls in and out, voicemail, contacts that work offline, notifications, PIN lock; sign in with your browser and it connects by itself.

Faxes →

Receive and send faxes on your ordinary numbers: PDFs by email, in My Phone and the app, with your own cover-page branding.

Store →

A public sign-up page: customers pick a plan and number, you approve, and only then are they charged and set up.

Single sign-on →

Sign in to the web panel with Authentik, Keycloak or any OpenID Connect provider; the password form stays as a fallback.

API →

Provision extensions, read live calls and sign-ins, and flip the kill switch or safety lock from your own scripts — plus the JSON API the Android app uses.

#How it fits together

                     Internet
                        │
          TCP 5061 (TLS) + UDP 20000-20099 (SRTP audio)
                        │
                 ┌──────▼───────┐      Router forwards ONLY these to the edge.
                 │  Edge server │      Kamailio (SIP proxy) + rtpengine (audio relay)
                 │ 192.168.1.11 │      fail2ban, flood limit, TLS certificate
                 └──────┬───────┘
                        │  plain SIP/RTP on your LAN
                 ┌──────▼───────┐
  Office phones ─►  PBX server  │      Asterisk 22 (calls, audio, voicemail)
  (UDP 5060)     │ 192.168.1.10 │      pbx-brain  (Python: every call decision)
                 │              │      pbx-agi    (Python: texts)
                 │              │      pbx-api    (Python: web panel, port 8001)
                 └──────────────┘      SQLite database /var/lib/pbx/pbx.db
                        │
                 SIP trunk provider (outbound registration)
  • Asterisk handles the low-level work: SIP, audio, recording, playing sounds. It has almost no dialplan — every call is handed to pbx-brain.
  • pbx-brain decides what happens to every call: who rings, forwarding, voicemail, ring groups, IVR, 911, plans and blocking.
  • pbx-api is the web panel and REST API. It owns the database and rebuilds Asterisk's phone list whenever you change users or trunks.
  • The edge server is optional. Without it the system works fully inside your network; you only need it for phones away from home.
